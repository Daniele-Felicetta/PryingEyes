# Track racing (Moto su pista)

The third way to play, chosen in the menu next to the standard game and the desert: a motorcycle race on a circuit that the game draws from a
seed, against computer riders or with up to three friends, on a physics model that behaves like a real bike (it leans, slides, wheelies, runs wide
and falls), with a garage of nine bikes in six classes (a showroom where the chosen bike turns on a plate beside seven bars of figures measured on the physics, and a choice of colours), three riding styles, a body that shifts on the seat, a slipstream, bikes that touch and fall, the best lap kept as a ghost, twelve
named circuits, a map that shows every rider as a dot, a driving line on the road (green, yellow and red) that says where to brake and a sign on the dashboard that counts down to it, a television replay of the race with a camera for the photographs, nine licence tests with bronze, silver and gold medals, a championship of three cups (four rounds each, the same eleven rivals all season, a table of points and trophies) and, as an option, a career with money (bikes to buy and tune, prizes for races, medals and cups). The goal that started it was "the
closest thing to Tourist Trophy": a simulation to be learned, not the arcade riding of the desert's motorbike, which is left as it is ([the-desert.md](the-desert.md#the-motorbike)).

Written on 21 November 2026 on the branch `claude/moto-pista-mode-1435d9`, in thirteen rounds the same day (the mode, then everything that came after it with the
aim of being as close to Tourist Trophy as it can get, then the licence tests, the garage, the championship, the rider's outfit, the keyboard, the contact between bikes, the bikes and the rider drawn again, the career with money, its workshop, the driving line and the braking sign). Nobody has ridden it by hand yet: every statement below comes from the code, from the rules lab and from the
headless runs listed under "How it is checked", and what no run covers says "(not verified)".

## How to play

`Gioca` asks how to play with three modes: the standard game, `Il deserto` and `Su pista`. `Su pista` opens a setup panel with a picture of the
circuit that will be raced; `Altra pista` draws another one. The rows are remembered between sessions.

| Row | Choices |
| --- | --- |
| `Circuito` | `Casuale` (a circuit of one kind, from a seed: nothing about it is remembered) or one of the twelve named circuits (2.5 to 4.1 km, each with a line about its character: smooth, fast, technical, hilly) |
| `Giri` | 1, 2, 3 (default), 5, 8 |
| `Avversari` | 0 (a timed lap alone), 3, 7, 11 (default), 15 |
| `Livello` | `Principiante`, `Esperto` (default), `Campione`: how fast the computer riders go and how often they overdo a corner |
| `Moto` | the nine bikes of "The garage" (the garage screen has the same row); with the career on, only the bikes owned (see "The career") |
| `Stile di guida` | `Peso fuori`, `Neutro` (default), `Moto in piega` |
| `Aiuti alla guida` | `Completi` (default: ABS, traction control, anti-wheelie, anti-stoppie, automatic gears, lean assist, which keeps the lean at the grip limit: a key is all or nothing), `Sportivi` (ABS, traction control, anti-wheelie, anti-stoppie, automatic gears; the lean is yours), `Nessuno` (only ABS; manual gears `E` / `Q`); the driving line on the road follows the level (see "The driving line"): all three colours with `Completi`, only the red with `Sportivi`, none with `Nessuno`; the braking sign on the dashboard comes and goes with the red |
| `Ora del giorno` | `Mattino`, `Mezzogiorno` (default), `Pomeriggio`: the sky of the desert, frozen at that hour |

`Campionato` at the top left opens the championship (see "The championship"), `Patenti` at the top right the licence tests (see below) and `Garage`, just under it, the garage screen (see "The garage"; the career is switched on there, see "The career"). `Via` starts (the button reads `Via`: the font of the menu draws an exclamation mark like an `l`). From the hosting path (`Ospita una partita`) the same panel opens, without `Campionato` and `Patenti` (both are for one player) and with every bike free whatever the career says, and `Via` opens the door for friends instead (see "Co-op").
Split screen on one PC does not offer this mode: its button is disabled there.

A race is: the grid and a flight of the camera, five red lights one second apart, then dark after a random hold; laps counted by timing loops every
50 m; a fall that is cleared by itself (the rider is put back on the asphalt facing the race) or with `R`; the end when every person has finished,
or at the latest 45 s after the first rider crossed the line (whoever is still out there is ranked by the distance ridden), then a table of results.
On the results `INVIO` / `A` races again on the same circuit, `N` / `X` on a new one, `T` / `Y` watches the replay, `ESC` / `START` goes back to the menu.

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Throttle | `W` / Up | right trigger |
| Front brake / rear brake | `S` / Down, `Space` | left trigger, `X` |
| Lean in and out of the corners | `A` `D` / Left Right | left stick |
| Shift the body (forward and back, left and right) | `I` `K`, `J` `L` | right stick |
| Tuck behind the screen | `Shift` | `A` |
| Gears (only with `Nessuno`) | `E` up, `Q` down | `RB`, `LB` |
| Camera (behind, far, helmet) | `C` | `Y` |
| Look back (held) | `V` | (none) |
| The ghost, on and off | `G` | (none) |
| Back on the track after a fall | `R` | `B` |

The keys ramp the way a hand does (the throttle opens in a third of a second, the front brake builds up, the steering grows the longer the key is held
and is slower the faster the bike goes), so a tap is a small correction and not a fall. A key is all or nothing, so above 36 km/h the steering keys reach only part of the lean: the share falls from the whole lean at 10 m/s (36 km/h) to 70% at 25 m/s (90 km/h) and stays there (the controller's stick is analog and is not capped). The ramps and the cap are `Pista/KeyShaper.cs`, which has no Unity in it, so the lab rides with the same code. The screen shows the speed in km/h with the gear and a bar of
lights for the engine speed, `SCIA` when another bike is sheltering you from the wind, the position, the lap and the times (the best and the last), the gap to the rider ahead and behind, the gap to the ghost,
the five lights, messages in the middle (`CADUTO`, `CONTROMANO`, a new record), and in a corner the map of the circuit with a dot per rider.

## The garage

Nine machines, from a 125 two-stroke that weighs as much as its rider to a 1300 that tops 300 km/h. Their numbers are those of real bikes of the class (mass, power curve, gearing, tyres), not those of a game;
the lab measured them on 21 November 2026 on the same physics, on flat asphalt, alone:

| Bike | Class | Engine | Power | Mass (with rider) | Top speed | 0-100 km/h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Argo 1000 | Superbike | inline four | 190 hp at 13,000 rpm | 275 kg | 296 km/h | 3.29 s |
| Argo 600 | Supersport | inline four | 129 hp at 13,500 rpm | 262 kg | 265 km/h | 3.77 s |
| Vigile 750 | Roadster | inline four | 112 hp at 10,500 rpm | 280 kg | 216 km/h | 4.05 s |
| Scintilla 125 | 125 GP | two-stroke single | 50 hp at 11,500 rpm | 133 kg | 207 km/h | 4.23 s |
| Tigre 675 | Supersport | triple | 126 hp at 11,500 rpm | 256 kg | 253 km/h | 3.48 s |
| Duca 1200 | Superbike | V twin | 187 hp at 9,500 rpm | 267 kg | 283 km/h | 3.22 s |
| Gigante 1300 | Hypersport | inline four | 195 hp at 9,800 rpm | 305 kg | 304 km/h | 3.17 s |
| Rombo 500 | GP 500 | two-stroke V4 | 190 hp at 12,000 rpm | 198 kg | 297 km/h | 3.17 s |
| Corsaro 1000 | Roadster | inline four | 161 hp at 11,000 rpm | 261 kg | 240 km/h | 3.43 s |

Every bike has its own livery and one of three body shapes, and a size (the 125 is drawn at 90 %, the 1300 at 106 %). **The rivals ride the class of the player's bike**, so a race is between machines that are a match for each other,
and the grid puts the quickest of the class in front. In the lab every bike rides two laps alone on two circuits without a fall.

**The garage screen.** `Garage` (under `Patenti` in the setup panel) opens a showroom: the chosen bike with its rider, turning on a plate at 24 degrees a second, its name and class, a sentence about it, and seven bars with their figures. The figures are not written by hand: `BikeSheet` rides every bike once on flat asphalt with the real model and reads the power (in hp), the torque (in Nm), the top speed, the time from 0 to 100 km/h, the distance from 100 km/h to a stop, the mass and a word for the handling (`Pesante`, `Stabile`, `Maneggevole`, `Agilissima`: from how fast the bike rolls into a corner and how much grip its tyres give). The bars have fixed scales, never relative to the other bikes, so that a bar means the same on every screen; the lab checks that each bar spreads over at least a quarter of its length across the nine bikes and that no two bikes draw nearly the same picture (the torque is a bar of its own because the 1000 four and the 1200 twin, with the same power and top speed, drew almost the same picture without it).
`Moto` steps the bike (it is the row of the setup), `Colori` the colours: `Di serie` (the livery the bike comes in) or one of the twelve liveries of the game, worn by the bike on the plate and shown as two swatches, and `Pilota` the rider's outfit: `Come la moto` (the leathers and the helmet of the bike's livery) or the leathers and the helmet of one of the twelve liveries, worn by the rider on the plate and shown as three swatches (the leathers, their second tone, the helmet) while the paint of the bike stays as chosen. The race is ridden in the colours and the outfit chosen (they are remembered with the other rows), the computer riders never take the colours, and the player's own rider wears the outfit in the race, in the replay and in the ghost; a friend in a co-op game sees the colours of the bike (the outfit is not sent). `-pistaoutfit N` sets it in an automated run (-1 the bike's own). `Fatto` (`Esc`, `B`) goes back to the setup with the `Garage` button still chosen. The button at the top right, `Carriera`, switches the career on and off (see "The career"): with it on the plate also shows the bikes that are not owned, with their price, and a `Compra` button. The showroom is a camera of its own with its own lights, drawn into a texture far below the scene, and it draws only while the screen is open.

**The engines have voices.** The five kinds of engine sound different, and none is a recording: `EngineSynth` (pure code, measured in the lab) builds each as a train of firing pulses that excite a few resonances of the exhaust, plus
a texture of growl, hiss and rumble, in a loop that closes on itself without a click; the game plays it faster or slower with the engine speed. The centres of the spectrum of the four made this way are 516 Hz (the twin: dark and fat), 846 (the triple), 1,787
(the two-stroke single) and 2,673 Hz (the two-stroke V4: a rasp); the inline four is the sound of the first version. Whether they *sound* like those engines is not verified, because nobody listened.

## The career

Tourist Trophy has a career: a player who starts with little wins races and licences and buys the bikes that are out of reach at first. Here it is **an option, off by default**: `Carriera`, at the top right of the garage screen, switches it on and off (the switch and the book are kept in the preferences; `-pistacareer 1` turns it on in an automated run). With it off the mode is what it always was: all nine bikes are the player's, nothing is paid and no table says a word about money. With it on, in a game alone:

- **The start** is 3,000 credits (`cr`) and one bike, the Scintilla 125, the cheapest. The book (`CareerBook` in `Career.cs`) is the money, a mask of the bikes owned and a digit of tuning for each bike, written to the preferences as one short text (`m=3000;o=8;u=000000000`; a book from before the workshop has no `u` and reads as every bike as it came) under `PryingEyes.Pista.CareerBook` every time something changes; it reads back what it wrote and shrugs off damaged text (a book that cannot be read is a new career, and the starter bike is always owned). Switching the career off and on again keeps the book.
- **The prices** are a table of the game, not worked out from the figures of the bikes (so a change of the physics does not move them): Scintilla 125 3,500 (the starter, owned from the first day), Vigile 750 8,000, Argo 600 8,500, Tigre 675 9,500, Corsaro 1000 10,500, Rombo 500 14,000, Argo 1000 14,500, Duca 1200 15,500 and Gigante 1300 18,500; the eight bikes to buy cost 99,000 in all. In the garage the `Moto` row still goes through all nine, to look at them: the plate, the bars and the figures are the bike's, and the name line says `tua` or the price (`Tigre 675  -  Supersport  -  9.500 cr`). The bike chosen for the race follows the row only while it is owned. Under the plate, for a bike that is not owned, `Compra  -  9.500 cr` buys it if the money is there (the money goes down by the price, the bike is the one chosen and the line above the button says `Comprata: Tigre 675  -  conto 500 cr`) and says what is missing otherwise (`Ti mancano 6.500 cr per la Tigre 675`). The cross reaches it between `Pilota` and `Fatto`.
- **The setup panel** says the money after its title (`Carriera: conto 500 cr`) and its `Moto` row steps only through the bikes owned; a choice made before the career, of a bike that is not owned, is read as the first bike that is.
- **A race pays.** The purse for the winner is 60 cr a lap, times (1 + the riders against the player / 6), times (1 + the price of the bike / 12,000), times 0.8, 1.0 or 1.3 for `Principiante`, `Esperto` or `Campione`, rounded to tens; a place takes a share of it (1st 100 %, 2nd 60, 3rd 40, 4th 28, 5th 20, 6th 14, 7th 10, 8th 7, 9th 5, 10th 4, 11th 3, 12th 2). Whoever does not cross the line, comes after the twelfth place or rides a race against fewer than three riders (that is practice) gets nothing. Three laps against eleven experts on the Scintilla 125 pay 660 for the win, 400 for the second place, 260 for the third and 10 for the twelfth (530 for a win at `Principiante`, 860 at `Campione`); the same race on the Gigante 1300 pays 1,300 for the win. The prize is paid once, when the table of results comes up (the replay and the way back do not pay it again), goes into the book at once and is said at the end of the line under the title of the table: `Autodromo del Faro  -  3 giri  -  premio 1.500 cr`.
- **A licence test pays its medals**: bronze 300, silver 700, gold 1,500 in all for a test (a better medal on the same test pays only the difference, so climbing by steps pays what the top medal pays at once, and a worse or equal lap pays nothing), and another 1,000 the first time a licence is obtained (a medal in each of its three tests). All nine tests at gold are 16,500 (9 x 1,500 and 3 x 1,000), a sixth of what the eight bikes cost. The test lends its bike: it does not have to be owned.
- **The cups pay as races, with a trophy on top.** Each round pays like a race (the laps and the level of the season, the bike of the cup) and a finished season adds its trophy: 2,000 for bronze, 3,500 for silver, 6,000 for gold. **A cup is ridden on a bike of its class that the player owns**: the one of the setup panel if it is of the class, else the first owned of that class. With none the cup cannot be started: its line says `Serve una moto della classe Superbike: comprane una nel garage (Carriera)`, `Invio` says `Non si parte: nella carriera ti serve una moto di questa classe` and a click does nothing. (With the career off the cup takes the first bike of its class, as it always did.)
- **The workshop** (`Tuning.cs`): a bike that is owned goes up three levels of tuning, and a level is a package: +4 % torque (so +4 % power), +2 % grip on both tyres, 2 % less weight (of the bike, not of the rider) and 3 % less drag; at the top that is +12 %, +6 %, -6 % and -9 %. A level costs a share of the price of the bike, 8 %, 13 % and 20 % in whole tens, so a full tune is 41 % of it (on the Tigre 675: 760, 1,240 and 1,900; from 1,440 on the Scintilla 125 to 7,590 on the Gigante 1300). In the garage `Potenzia  -  760 cr` takes the place of `Compra` under the plate of a bike that is owned and not yet at the top, with the same place in the cross; the line above it says what the next level gives (`Livello 1:   +4 % potenza   +2 % aderenza   -2 % peso   -3 % aria`) until something else is said (`Potenziata: Tigre 675, livello 1  -  conto 4.740 cr`, or `Ti mancano 260 cr per il livello 1 della Tigre 675`). The name line says the level (`tua  -  livello 1`), the sentence of the bike gets a line (`Messa a punto 1 di 3:   ...`), and the bars and the figures are those of the bike as it is tuned (`BikeSheet.Of(index, level)` rides the tuned bike on the model). At the top level the button is gone. **A tuned bike is a copy** (`Tuning.Apply`: the torque curve is copied and the roster is never touched) **whose key says the level** (`argo600+2`), and whatever is kept by key keeps the levels apart: the curves of the computer riders (`BikeCurves`), the racing line of a race (`Race.LineFor`) and the ghosts and records of the best laps (`PistaDirector.BikeId`: a record set tuned is not one set on the bike as it comes, and the setup panel says `Record con Tigre 675 liv. 2`). The race takes the tuned bike for the player in a free race and in a cup while the career applies (`PistaMode.SpecFor`); a licence test lends the bike as it comes, even one that is owned and tuned, a co-op game ignores the career (the bike is as it comes), and the rivals are never tuned. The banner of the start, the setup panel and the cup panel name the level (`Tigre 675 liv. 2`). What a full tune is worth was measured in the lab, not felt: 0.3 to 2.2 % off the planned lap of the computer riders (their plan uses a fixed grip, so it only feels the power, the weight and the drag; 0.6 to 2.3 % in a ridden lap) and 2.4 to 3.9 % for a rider who uses the grip of the tune too, about a third of the spread between the computer riders. The grip is worth most: +5 % of it alone takes 1.6 % off the lap, +10 % of torque 0.7 %, -10 % of weight 0.3 % and -10 % of drag 0.1 %.
- **A cooperative game ignores the career**: the panel of the host steps through all nine bikes and says nothing of money (the garage there has no `Carriera` button), the friends ride the bike of the host, and nothing is paid on any machine.

The numbers are mine. The rules lab (`career` group) only keeps them from drifting: every bike has a price and a bigger class costs more, the cheapest step up (the Vigile 750 at 8,000) is within reach of three golds in the first licence (3,000 + 3 x 1,500 + 1,000 = 8,500), a win is 3 to 12 % of it (8.2 %: about a dozen wins) and all nine licences together (16,500) do not buy the eight bikes, so the rest is won on the track.
The code is `Pista/Career.cs` (pure: `Economy`, with the prices, the purses, the prizes and the prices of the workshop, and `CareerBook`, which also holds the levels), `Tuning.cs` (pure: the three levels and the tuned copy of a bike), `PistaCareer.cs` (the book in the preferences), `PistaDirector.Career.cs` (what the table of results pays), `PistaMode.cs` (`CareerOn`, and `CareerApplies`: on, and neither in a co-op game nor in the panel that hosts one) and, in the menu, `GameMenu.Garage.cs`, `GameMenu.Pista.cs` and `GameMenu.Cup.cs`. What the career does not have: selling a bike, parts one by one (the tuning is three packages), a way to begin again from the menu (the book is the preference `PryingEyes.Pista.CareerBook`), and anything for the rivals (they neither earn nor buy).

## The bike

`BikeModel` is a rigid frame that leans, an engine with a gearbox, two wheels with slip, tyres that share their grip between braking, driving and cornering
(the friction ellipse), weight that moves between the wheels, air drag and the road underneath. It is deterministic: the same inputs give the same ride on every
machine. The laws, in the order the code applies them:

1. The rider asks for a path curvature; the bike leans to the angle that curvature needs at this speed, tan(lean) = v² k / g, rolling there with a limited roll rate;
   the rider's body shift can carry the centre of gravity beyond the frame's own lean.
2. The yaw rate follows the lean, r = g tan(lean) / v, the textbook rule of a motorcycle.
3. The tyres must supply m v r of side force. Each has mu N of grip, shared with its braking or driving force. Past the limit the front pushes wide (and tucks if the rider keeps on),
   the rear steps out (and highsides if the grip comes back).
4. Along the bike: the torque curve, gears, rev limiter and slipping clutch at launch, wheel inertia, brakes with optional ABS, traction control and anti-wheelie, drag, rolling resistance, slope.
5. Weight moves from wheel to wheel with the acceleration, m a h / L: a light front rotates the bike about the rear tyre (wheelie), and the other way round for a stoppie.
6. A crest unloads the tyres (u² times the vertical curvature) and a dip loads them; kerbs and gravel shake; barriers stop the bike and above a limit knock the rider off.

The physics runs at a fixed 60 steps a second and the bikes are drawn between the last two steps, so any refresh rate looks smooth. What the rules lab measured on the Argo 1000:
100 km/h to a stop in 39.8 m (1.35 g) and 200 km/h in 156 m; a steady turn at 90 km/h on a 70 m radius needs 42.3° of lean (36.7° the frame, 5.8° the body). The numbers are those of a real machine of the class;
whether they *feel* like Tourist Trophy is not verified.

## The driving line

With the aids at `Completi` the road says what to do, the way Gran Turismo does: a narrow ribbon (42 cm wide) floats 6 cm above the asphalt along the racing line the computer riders follow, from 8 m behind the bike to 300 m ahead of it, in three colours. **Green** (the plan has room over the bike): accelerate; a bike standing on the grid sees nothing else. **Yellow**: the bike is at the speed the plan has there, hold it or ease off through the corner; it also comes just before the red, like the amber of a traffic light, because the plan falls through the speed of the bike on its way down. **Red**: the plan slows to under the speed of the bike, brake. The red begins about where a rider at that speed would have to start braking (where the plan is 3 % under the speed of the bike, drawn 12 m earlier), so a faster bike sees it earlier and a slower one later, and it shrinks as the bike brakes: its near end moves towards the corner (the places that are now at the speed of the bike turn yellow) and it is gone when the bike is down to within about a tenth of the speed of the corner.

What the aids let the rider see: `Completi` all three colours, `Sportivi` only the red (the marks of the braking zones), `Nessuno` no line at all. A licence test has the aids of the test (see "The licence tests") and in a cooperative game they are the host's. The line goes away with the race: it is hidden on the table of results and in the replay.

**The braking sign.** The ribbon has a weakness that the photographs of its first version showed: from the chase camera the bike and the rider hide it where it runs straight under them (at 96 km/h the red ahead was behind the rider, and at 202 km/h the red 78 m ahead was a thin orange sliver at the horizon), and from the far camera or the helmet it is a thin strip. So the same advice is also said on the dashboard, where no camera hides it: a sign at the bottom of the screen, a little left of the middle and clear of the weight dot, that is up only while a braking zone is near or on. While the red is still ahead and within five seconds of road at the speed of the bike (never under 60 m and never past the 300 m of the ribbon) it counts down, `FRENA TRA 200 m` (the metres to the nearest 10 from 100 m and to the nearest 5 under that), in amber that turns red as the red comes, over a bar that fills. When the bike is on the red it says `FRENA` in a pulsing red and the bar shows how much of the red is left. Under the words it says the speed the braking is for, `fino a 155 km/h`: the lowest speed of the plan in the first dip after the start of the red, to the nearest 5 km/h (left out when the plan only keeps falling over the 800 m it is read). It says nothing for green and yellow (it is about the brakes), nothing for a bike that stands still, nothing while the bike is down, over the table of results or after the finish line, and the aids decide whether there is one as they decide the red of the ribbon: `Completi` and `Sportivi` have it, `Nessuno` has none. The hints of the start (bottom left) move up to make room for it.

How it is worked out (`Pista/DrivingLine.cs` and `Pista/BrakeCue.cs`, pure code that the lab compiles; `PistaGuide.cs` draws the ribbon and `PistaHud.cs` the sign):

- **The plan** is the speed plan of the racing line (`RacingLine.vplan`) made for the bike that is ridden, so a tuned bike has a line of its own and a faster bike brakes longer: on the twelve named circuits the Gigante 1300 has 762 m of red a lap against 557 m for the Scintilla 125.
- **The rule** (`DrivingLine.Advise`, at every sample of the stretch, every frame): with `r` the speed of the plan at a place over the speed of the bike, the place is red when `r` is under 0.97, green when it is over 1.05, and in between it is what a rider on the plan does there (a braking zone the bike is not up to yet is yellow: hold the speed). A bike under 1 m/s sees green all the way.
- **The stretch the player sees** (`DrivingLine.Window`): the advice of every sample, then three clean-ups. A red that never gets deeper than 90 % of the speed of the bike is yellow (a fast kink of the plan, taken without touching the brakes, is not a braking zone: the first version drew 16 m of red at a kink where the plan fell 3.4 % under the speed of the bike, which a photograph showed and the lab had not); the red that is left starts 12 m earlier; and a piece of advice shorter than 10 m in the middle of the stretch is folded into its longer neighbour (`Tidy`), so that a blip of the plan, a few metres where its curve changes shape, is not a speck on the road. The pieces at the two ends of the stretch are kept as they are, because the stretch cuts them.
- **What a rider on the plan does** (`DrivingLine.Classify`): brake where the plan slows the bike by more than 3.5 m/s2 (a little over a third of a g), accelerate where it speeds it up by more than 0.4 m/s2 or is within 3 % of the top speed of the plan, hold the speed elsewhere, with the same lead and the same minimum piece. A lap of the named circuits has 5 to 14 such zones and 13.5 to 27 % of its length in red.
- **The ribbon** is one strip of two vertices per sample of the lap (every 2 m), made once per race with the same height function as the road and the physics, so it follows the camber and the crests; every frame only the triangles of the stretch ahead are given to its three materials, pure hues so that the sun and the glow do not turn the red orange.
- **The sign** (`Pista/BrakeCue.cs`, pure code that the lab compiles; `PistaHud.SetCue` draws it): `DrivingLine.FirstBraking` takes the stretch the ribbon shows, finds the first red from the bike on (where it begins and how long it is) and then reads the plan itself, not the stretch, for the speed of the corner: from the start of the red down to its lowest point, the bottom of the first dip, which ends where the plan has climbed 5 % above it again, within 800 m (`ScanMetres`). `BrakeCues.Make` turns that into the sign for the aids and the speed of the bike. The sign and the ribbon are made from the same advice, so the sign never says what the road does not: the test in the game compares them on every frame of a race (about a hundred thousand frames, none that differ), and the friend's machine in the co-op run does the same with the host's aids.

The figures (the 3.5 m/s2, the 0.97 and the 1.05, the 90 %, the 12 m, the 10 m, the 300 m, the width, and for the sign the five seconds, the 60 m, the rounding, the 5 % and the 800 m) are mine: the lab checks that the line does what is said above, not that a person finds it right. It shows the plan of the computer riders, which is not necessarily the best a person can do.

## The rider

The body of the rider is part of the model, not a picture on top of it.

- **The riding style** (`Stile di guida`, the way the weight sits in a corner): `Peso fuori` hangs the body out and keeps the bike more upright (25° of lean reached in 0.242 s, 32.4° of frame in the 70 m corner at 90 km/h, a bit more grip at full lock),
  `Moto in piega` leans the bike under a body that stays on it (0.300 s, 42.3°, less grip at depth), `Neutro` is in between (0.267 s, 36.7°). The numbers are the lab's.
- **The body shift** (`I J K L`, the right stick): forward loads the front wheel (the lightest the front gets in a launch: 498 N forward, 190 N in the middle, 41 N back) and back loads the rear under braking (409 N forward, 520 N middle, 620 N back); sideways moves the centre of gravity by up to 6.8 cm
  of a 140 cm wheelbase. Without keys the rider does it by himself.
- **The slipstream**: a bike within about 60 m behind another, in its line and not too slow, is sheltered from the wind (0.90 at 5 m, 0.62 at 18 m, nothing at 60 m, nothing from the side, from a rider who has fallen or head on) and up to a third comes off its drag; the
  `SCIA` light shows it. In the lab a grid of ten for 140 s spends a third of its time sheltered (33 %), so packs form.

## The circuit

`CircuitGen` makes a circuit from a seed (the same seed gives the same circuit on every machine, so a host only has to send a number): a main straight with the start line and room for the grid
behind it, then seven to eleven groups of corners (a single corner from a kink to a hairpin, a chicane, an esse, a double apex that tightens) joined by straights, scaled so that the lap turns exactly
360°, with hills (flat at the grid), camber, kerbs, paved run-off, gravel traps and a barrier. Over 40 seeds the lab sees laps of 2.45 to 4.15 km, closed to under a centimetre, at most 8 % grade, at least 1.2 m
of run-off everywhere and no two roads closer than 6 m; every one of the twenty grid places is on flat straight asphalt.

**The named circuits** (`Catalogue`) are twelve seeds picked by scanning a range of them (`tools/tracklab --scan FROM TO`) for their character, from the 2,508 m short, fast and hilly one to the 4,139 m long one, so that a record, a ghost
and a time mean something: a circuit is the same every time. The lab checks that every one still has the length listed (a change to the generator that moves one must be a decision, because it would orphan the records)
and that the list covers fast, technical and hilly ones (5, 7 and 5). `Autodromo di Montesole`, seed 4242, 2,732 m, is the first, the one to begin with.

The ground under the wheels is not the meshes of the scenery: the physics reads `Circuit.SurfaceY` and the surface (asphalt, kerb, apron, gravel, grass), and the ribbon of the road is drawn from the same
function, so what the wheels touch is what the eye sees. Around it `PistaWorld` builds, a few pieces per frame behind the loading screen: the road, the guard rails, the countryside as a height field, belts
of trees, the pit building, a grandstand, the starting gantry with its five lights, braking boards and a ring of hills; the sky is the desert's, frozen at the chosen hour.

Two faults of that scenery were found only when the replay put a camera where the bikes are not (21 November 2026), and are fixed:

- **A third of the lap was under the grass.** The plain that carries the countryside to the horizon was one flat sheet at a height of its own, under the whole terrain, and on a circuit with hills (the road of seed 4242 runs from -7.4 m to +10.3 m) every stretch of road lower than the average
  height minus two metres, 549 of the 1,366 samples of that lap, was *under* the sheet: no asphalt, no kerbs, the wheels sunk in the lawn. The plain is now a frame around the terrain, level with its edge, and the in-game test asks that no point of the road (the middle and both edges)
  lies under a piece of it. From above the whole ribbon is now there (`giro_0_dall_alto.png` of the photographs).
- **A wall of green streaks along the barriers.** The skirt that closes the crack between the ribbon and the ground had a plan-view texture smeared down a vertical face; it has texture coordinates of its own, along the wall and down it.

## The look of the world

The ground, the trees, the hills and the haze were redrawn after 0.10.0 (rounds 14 to 16 of the work towards a more modern look). It is judged **only by photographs** and by the time a frame takes: nobody has played a race on it yet, and "looks like a modern game" is a direction, not a result.

- **The ground** (`PistaSurface.shader`, one lit shader with five kinds picked by a material property). *Asphalt* has the grain of an aggregate with stones standing out of it, patches of other asphalt every 37 m, a dark groove of rubber that follows the line the bikes really take (the road ribbon carries a second set of coordinates: the lateral offset, the distance along the lap, the offset of the rubbered line there and the half width; the offset of the line comes from the curvature of the circuit: out to the apex, back before the corner and after it, `RubberLine`), marbles of rubber at the edges, a dusting at the edge and hairline cracks. *Paved run-off*, *gravel* (stones of four colours with relief, from a Voronoi texture), *grass* (three scales of tone, so that neither the tiling nor a flat green shows) and *kerbs* (the red and the white, scuffed) are the other kinds. The two detail textures (`PistaDetail.cs`: the grain and the stones, 512 by 512 with a normal in each) are made by pure arithmetic on a worker thread while the mode loads and shared by every race. Without them, or on a machine whose shader is not supported, the old flat textures are used.
- **The trees** (`PistaFoliage.cs`, `PistaFoliage.shader`, built in `PistaWorld.Props.cs`). A broadleaf tree is a trunk, four limbs and 24 to 31 cards of leaves laid in a squashed ball; a fir is a stem with nine tiers of fronds (ten cards round the bottom tier, six round the top), each card a trough that droops from the stem, and a leader of three upright cards. The two card textures are drawn by code (a cluster of 640 small leaves with a vein, 512 by 512, and a frond with its branchlets and needles, 256 by 256) with mipmaps made by hand so that a tree seen from far keeps the share of its leaves (an alpha-tested texture thins out when it is averaged). The normals of a card are those of the crown (away from its middle), so a tree is lit like a ball of leaves and not like a pile of paper; the colour of a vertex carries the tint of the tree and how dark it is inside the crown, and its alpha how much the card sways in the wind. The shader cuts the card by the texture in the colour, shadow and depth passes (so the shadow on the grass is a tree and not a set of squares), lets the sun through the leaf when it is behind it, and shades like the other materials (cloud shadows, haze).
- **The hills** (`BuildHills`): three ranges round the countryside, each a height field on a ring (so the sun shades its slopes and the ranges overlap), painted by a wrapping texture of fields and woods, the farthest almost the colour of the sky.
- **The trackside** (`PistaSigns.cs`, `PistaWorld.Trackside.cs`, put together in `BuildProps`). *Advertising boards*: sixteen designs of 256 by 96 texels (invented names in a five-by-seven letter table on a coloured ground with a stripe and a flash at each end) in one atlas drawn by code, two on every piece of the pit wall (they replace its white band) and seven on posts at the outside of the sharpest corners, set back along the road so that a bike coming to the corner reads them. *Spectators*: one picture of three rows of seated people, cut out by the leaf shader without light through it, on a card on each tier of the grandstand and on four tiers of three small stands at the outside of up to five corners; they are cards that do not move (only a hint of wind in the vertex colour). *Tyre barriers* behind the guard rail on the outside of the five sharpest corners (a heptagonal prism per tyre, some seven hundred a wall, the three upper ones painted white and red in threes) and *marshal posts* (a booth with a window and a door and a pole with a yellow flag) at the eight sharper ones. All of it stands only where the road leaves room (`Clear`) and none of it is solid: the physics knows only the limit of the circuit. Without the picture there are no boards (the pit wall keeps its band) and without the cut-out shader no crowd; the test counts what was built (`BoardCount`, `CrowdCards`, `TyreWalls`, `MarshalPosts`) and checks that the meshes are there with coordinates inside the atlas.
- **The sky and the haze** (`PistaLook.cs`): the sky is the desert's (`DesertSky`), frozen at the hour of the race, but the circuit has its own numbers for it: a thinner haze (0.22 of the desert's), a clear and slightly deeper sky, a little more exposure, and a haze and horizon tinted blue by day (the desert's are the colour of sand), which fades toward dawn and dusk so that their warm palette stays. The desert has none of this: `DesertSky` keeps its own values and gets them back when the race ends. Every number can be changed from the command line (`-pistafog`, `-pistaskygain`, `-pistazenith`, `-pistacloud`, `-pistaexposure`, `-pistasun`, `-pistadaylight`, `-pistaglow`, `-pistahorizon r,g,b`, `-pistahazetint r,g,b`) to try a look on the photographs without building again.
- **What speed does to the picture** (`PistaFx.cs`, driven from `PistaCamera.TickFx`). The race camera gets a volume of its own (global, priority 100) the first time it is told a speed, and throws it away when the race ends: two overrides and nothing else (the haze, the grade and the exposure stay in the sky's volume). The pipeline's *motion blur* in the mode that knows the objects (`CameraAndObjects`, high quality: the bike the camera follows moves with it, has no motion of its own on the screen and stays sharp, while the road, the kerbs and the trees streak) and a *colour fringe* at the edges of the picture (0.12 at the most). Both grow with the speed of the bike that is followed: nothing up to 79 km/h, all of it from 281 km/h, smoothly in between (`ShareOf`), the level rising in about 0.6 s and falling in 0.4. The blur is worked out as a shutter and not as a strength: the override blurs over twice its intensity times the distance the picture moves between two frames, and in the mode that knows the objects its `clamp` does nothing (it only limits the camera alone), so a fixed intensity blurs a fast machine less than a slow one and lets the ground next to the camera streak across half the screen (the first try, at 0.6, did exactly that). The intensity is the time the shutter stays open (4.2 ms at full speed) divided by twice the game time of the frame being drawn, never above 0.5, so that the streak is as long as the picture moves in that time whatever the frame rate: 0.126 at 60 pictures a second, 0.5 from 300. A cut (another view, looking back, a put-back on the track, a jump of the replay) starts again from nothing (`Reset`: two frames without effects), so that no frame is smeared across a jump. How much of it there is, is the player's: it follows *Oscillazione visuale* (at zero the effects are off, and so is the camera's tremor).

The photographs are `-pistatest -pistaworldshots -pistalaps 1 -pistarivals 5 -seed 777 -pistashotdir NAME` (a graphics device, no `-nographics`): noon in three places on the circuit with five views each (from behind the bike, the asphalt close up, a kerb, the grass, the horizon) and the morning and the evening in two places, then a dozen of the trackside from the places the world names for each kind of thing (`PistaWorld.vantages`: the pit wall twice, the grandstand twice, two corner boards, two small stands, two tyre barriers, two marshal posts), 1920 by 1080 without the HUD, into `hotel_build/prove/pista/mondo/NAME/`.

The race photographs of `-pistatest -pistabot -pistashots` (`5_gara_inseguimento`, `6_gara_lontana`, `7_gara_casco` in `hotel_build/prove/pista/`) are taken as the player would see them: in real time at 60 pictures a second (the blur of a frame is the distance the picture moves in it, so a photograph is taken at the rate of a screen and not at the one this machine could make) and a dozen frames after every change of view, so that no cut is in the picture, and after one throwaway drawing (`Warm`: the first drawing into a texture after seconds of the camera drawing only to the screen shows a blur of its own, much heavier than the game's, which the next drawings and the same frame drawn twice do not; the first versions of these photographs were taken for the game's blur and were not). The same view is photographed with the effects of speed off, at half and at full (`5a_effetti_spenti`, `5b_effetti_meta`, `5c_effetti_pieni`, without the HUD: in a photograph the HUD is drawn through the camera and is blurred with the world, in the game it is not), and the log gives the speed, the level, the intensity of the blur, the time of the frame and how far the camera moved and turned since the frame before.

## The riders

A computer rider (`BikeDriver`) follows a racing line (`RacingLine`: the fastest line that stays on the asphalt, planned with the speeds the bike really delivers, measured by riding the model itself) by
pure pursuit, holds the planned speed with throttle and brakes, moves over to pass slower bikes and now and then overcooks a corner. It rides the same physics as the player. The lab sees six circuits of two laps
alone with no fall and a lap within 3 % of the plan, and a grid of twelve finishing in order.

Standings are by the same rules for everybody (`Race`): the finishers by their time, the others by the distance ridden, a rider who has left last. Gaps come from the timing loops.

## Contact between bikes

Bikes touch (`Race.Contacts`, `BikeModel.Shove`). A bike is three discs of 0.4 m (the two tyres and the middle) that cannot overlap. When the discs of two bikes do, the deepest pair gives the line between them: the two bikes are moved out by the overlap (the lighter one more: each gives way by the other's share of the total mass), their speeds along that line are exchanged with a little bounce (restitution 0.15, so momentum is kept: a lab check measures it), the frame wobbles and the heading is twisted, more the farther from the middle of the bike the touch was. What else happens depends on how fast the two come together along that line (`closing`):

| Closing speed | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| under 1.2 m/s | the two are pushed apart and nothing else |
| 1.2 to 6 m/s | a bump: speed is exchanged and the frame wobbles; the camera shakes, and from 2.5 m/s the screen says `CONTATTO` |
| 6 to 9.5 m/s | the bike that rode into the other (the one whose own motion is more of the closing) falls (`CrashKind.Collision`); the other is pushed and stays up |
| over 9.5 m/s | both fall |

Only a person's bike is touched: the player's, or a friend's on another machine. Computer riders still go through each other (they steer around each other, and a pile-up of computer riders would only make the results of a cup noisy). Each machine moves only the bikes it simulates, with the share the masses give it, so a friend's pose is never pushed and both machines agree on the outcome without sending anything. A rider who is down, who has finished, who has left the race or who has just been put back on the track (three seconds of grace, so that nobody is run over standing in the racing line) is not touched. The computer riders go round the bikes ahead of them, the player's included, and the computer driver that stands for the player in the automated runs does the same (`Race.Avoid`); a bike coming from behind is not seen, so overtaking is the player's job. Without that the stand-in rode through the pack and was touched forty times a race; with it, four to eight.

## The bikes and the rider on screen

The bikes are drawn by code (`Pista/PistaBikeMesh.cs`): three shapes (superbike, supersport, naked roadster) share their parts and a bike is built once per shape; the paint and the leathers are the livery of the rider. The look is the stylised one of the rest of the world (smooth lofted shapes, no photographic textures), and it is judged only by photographs. What a bike is made of:

- **The body**: a fairing that ends in front of the rider's knees (a nose with an intake and two light lenses, side vents, a windscreen that follows the slope up from it, a belly pan), a tank, a seat, a short tail with its light and the number plates on the side; the naked bike has a headlamp pod with its lens and a flyscreen instead. The engine is a crankcase with the cylinders leaning forward and their cooling fins, the head and its cover, the clutch and alternator covers and the radiator; it shows under the fairing and in full on the naked bike.
- **The exhaust**: two headers that join into a collector under the engine and a silencer that rises toward the tail, in titanium.
- **The rider**: a chest that leans forward with shoulders, a neck and a hump on the back, a helmet longer than wide with a chin bar, a visor and a low spoiler, arms and legs that taper, a fist round each grip and a boot shaped like a boot. The hump, the shoulder caps and the second colour of the leathers are the trim (the second colour of the livery), the knee sliders are the first.

Photographs of the bikes in the showroom (side, front, tail, rider, engine) come from `-pistamenutest -pistamenugarage -pistabikeshots` (`-pistabikes 0,2,3` picks the bikes) into `hotel_build/prove/pista/bici_N_vista.png`; the picture comes from `GarageStage.Snapshot`, and each bike is photographed in the livery it comes in with its own rider.

A frame costs little: `-pistatest -pistaperf` (with a graphics device) draws the race camera at 1920x1080 with twelve riders and reads back what a frame takes, and on the machine of the tests (an RTX 5060 Ti) it is 2.7 ms on the grid, 3.3 ms in the first ten seconds of the race (the pack is closest: 95% of those frames take under 5.2 ms, the worst 12 ms) and 2.7 ms later in the lap, with 135 to 290 objects and 180 to 340 thousand triangles in view: 300 to 370 frames a second. The models described above cost about a tenth more objects and triangles than the simple shapes they replaced (on the grid 262 objects and 311 thousand triangles before, 287 and 337 thousand after) and no time that the probe can see (2.7, 3.4 and 2.7 ms before), so there was room for more detail. The new look of the world (see "The look of the world") used some of it: with the ground shader, the trees made of cards, the shadows of the trees and the three ranges of hills, a frame takes 3.5 ms on the grid, 4.0 ms in the first ten seconds of the race (95% under 5.9 ms, the worst 15.8 ms) and 3.3 ms later in the lap, with 138 to 285 objects and 229 to 418 thousand triangles in view: 250 to 300 frames a second (measured on 21 November 2026, 11 PM, before the trackside boards and crowd). The trackside (boards, crowd cards, tyre barriers and marshal posts, see "The look of the world") added about 37 thousand triangles on the grid (455 thousand) and nothing the probe can see: in five runs on 22 November 2026, 12:19 to 12:24 AM, 3.5 ms on the grid, 3.9 to 4.1 ms in the first ten seconds (95% under 5.8 to 6.0 ms, the worst frame 14.4 to 18.0 ms) and 3.3 to 3.4 ms later in the lap: 245 to 301 frames a second. In the first two of those runs, made right after the long regression, one frame of 42 and one of 53 ms showed up in the last window; the next three, with the probe now logging any frame over 25 ms with its place on the lap, had none (the worst 10.8 to 11.4 ms), so it was taken for the machine and not for the scene, which is a guess. The effects of speed (`PistaFx`, see "The look of the world") are off on the grid and cost a little where they are on: one run on 22 November 2026, 1:17 AM, gave 3.5 ms on the grid, 4.3 ms in the first ten seconds (95% under 6.2 ms, the worst frame 16.3 ms) and 3.7 ms later in the lap, 0.2 to 0.4 ms above the five runs before (the camera's own drawing 3.5 and 3.1 ms against 3.3 and 2.9), which is the price of the high-quality motion blur and the fringe at 1920x1080 on this machine; it is one run and not five. It says nothing about a weaker machine.

## Timing, the ghost and the records

Every lap of the player is timed against itself: a trace (the clock against the distance, every ten metres) and a recording of the bike (a frame every 1/20 s). A lap that is over and clean (no fall, no cut) and is the best of this bike on this circuit becomes the
**ghost**, kept apart for the lap that starts from the grid and for a flying lap; in a race the ghost rides translucent on the circuit and the screen shows the gap to it (`G` turns it off and on). On a named circuit the ghost is also written to a file of its own under the game's data
folder (`pista/fantasma-<seed>-<bike>-<kind>.bin`), so it is still there tomorrow; a circuit of one kind remembers nothing. A damaged, truncated or foreign file is ignored (the lab feeds 213 truncated copies of a real one and none is accepted), and **the automated runs never read or write the
player's files**. A lap that ends in a fall is not kept.

## The licence tests

`Patenti`, the button at the top right of the setup panel, opens nine tests in three licences, named like the categories of a real motorcycle licence: **A1** (the small bikes: a 125, all the aids, at noon), **A2** (the middle ones, 600 to 750,
the sporty aids, in the morning) and **A** (the big ones, 1000 to 1300, the sporty aids, in the afternoon). Each test fixes the bike, the circuit (a named one), the aids, the hour of the day, three laps and nobody else on the track, and
they are not locked: any can be taken at any time. A click or `INVIO` starts it, and what the setup panel had chosen is put aside until the game is over. It is for one player: the button is not there when hosting a co-op game.

The lap that counts is the fastest **flying lap with no fall in it**. The first lap starts from the grid and never counts, and a lap with a fall is left out; with three laps there are two chances, so one mistake can be made up for. The three times to beat stay on the screen under the record line, each in the
colour of its medal; the banner at the end of a lap says the medal it earned (or why it did not count), and the table of results says `PROVA FINITA` with the medal in big letters, the three times, how many seconds would earn the next medal, the best time of the book and, when the third test of a licence has just got a medal, the licence (`PATENTE A1 OTTENUTA`).
On the results `INVIO` / `A` is the same test again (there is no new circuit to ask for). The best time of every test is kept in the preferences of the player, as one line of text, and the medal is worked out from it, so it follows the targets; the automated runs keep their book in memory only.
A licence is obtained when its three tests have a medal.

## The championship

`Campionato`, the button at the top left of the setup panel (across from `Patenti` and `Garage`), opens three cups, one for each class of bike that has a field to race against: **Coppa 125** (the 125 GP class: the Scintilla 125), **Coppa Supersport** (Argo 600, Tigre 675) and **Coppa Superbike** (Argo 1000, Duca 1200).
The other three classes (Roadster, Hypersport, GP 500) have no cup and stay for single races. A cup is **four rounds** on four of the named circuits, three laps each, against **the same eleven computer riders all season**: it is for one player, and the button is not there when hosting a co-op game.

| Cup | Class | Rounds (the circuit and its length) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `c125` Coppa 125 | 125 GP | Autodromo di Montesole 2.7 km, Pista di Valle Alta 2.8 km, Autodromo del Faro 2.5 km, Circuito del Vento 2.9 km |
| `csup` Coppa Supersport | Supersport | Circuito dei Tre Ponti 2.9 km, Anello dei Cipressi 2.9 km, Circuito della Torre 3.6 km, Pista del Vento 3.5 km |
| `csbk` Coppa Superbike | Superbike | Autodromo della Rocca 3.2 km, Pista della Gola 3.1 km, Anello del Pozzo 4.1 km, Pista di San Gaudenzio 4.1 km |

**The field.** The names, bikes and colours of the eleven come from the cup alone (a generator of our own, so that they are the same on every machine) and no rival wears the colours of the player's bike. Their pace is the level of the setup panel (`Livello`) *when the season began* plus a fixed share of a spread, so that a rival who is quick in the first round is quick in the last
and the standings mean something: for `Principiante` 0.885 of the speed that the racing line plans, a spread of 0.06 and a `mistakes` setting (how often a rider overdoes a corner) of 0.28; for `Esperto` 0.935, 0.045 and 0.12; for `Campione` 0.968, 0.027 and 0.04. Changing `Livello` in the middle of a season changes nothing until a new one begins.

**The points** are the usual ones of a motorcycle championship, 25, 20, 16, 13, 11, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 for the first fifteen places, and only for whoever crosses the line (twelve riders share 134 points a round, 536 a season). The standings are by points, then wins, then second places, then third places, then the order of the field with the player first.

**The panel.** Every cup is a button with the state of its season (`Stagione da cominciare`, `Gara 3 di 4 - 2° con 41 punti`, `Finita - 1° con 100 punti`) and the best trophy ever won in it. Under the cup chosen: its calendar (the rounds done, and the next one), the standings (before the first round, the eleven riders with their bikes and the player with his),
the bike (`In sella`: the one of the setup panel if it is of the class, else the first bike of the class) and the level of the rivals (fixed since the season began), and what `INVIO` does. A click or `INVIO` on a cup races the next round of its season, or the first round of a new season if there is none or the last one is over; the aids and the hour are the player's, the circuit, the laps and the rivals are the cup's.
`Ricomincia` appears when a season is in progress and, pressed a second time (the first only asks), throws it away and begins another at the level of the setup panel now. The panel opens on the cup in progress, else on the first cup never finished.

**The race.** Twelve riders: the player and the eleven; at the start the banner names the cup in the big line and the round, the circuit, the laps and the bike under it. The first round is gridded by pace (the quickest rival of the class on pole, the player in the middle), the others by the standings (the leader on pole). An ordinary race is over as soon as every *person* has crossed the line, and the computer riders still out are ranked by the distance they have ridden;
a cup's race **waits for the whole field** (`Race.waitForField`), because the points go to everyone who crosses the line: it is over when the last rider is in, or 45 s after the first, and the screen says `Gli altri stanno ancora arrivando` while the player waits (the first run of the in-game test found it: with the ordinary rules two riders in twelve crossed the line and nobody behind the player scored). When the table of results comes up the round counts for the season **once** (the replay and the way back to the table do not count it again) and four lines under the table say
what the round gave: the cup and the round, the place, the points and the place in the standings, the first three of the standings, and the next circuit (`INVIO` / `A` goes there: in a cup there is no "again" and no "new circuit") or, after the last round, `STAGIONE FINITA` with the place and the trophy: gold for the first place of the season, silver for the second, bronze for the third.
The best place of a finished season is kept for good, a new season does not take it away. The book is one line of text per cup in the preferences of the player, written after every round (a round that is not finished is not counted: leaving in the middle of one loses that round, not the season); the automated runs keep it in memory only.
`-pistacuplaps N` in an automated run shortens the rounds to `N` laps.

## Replay and photographs

When a race is over the results offer `T` (`Y`): the race is played back from a tape recorded while it was ridden (20 poses a second for every rider and the distance along the race as the rules saw it, at most 18,000 frames: fifteen minutes), and every bike stands where the tape says, at every frame, whatever the camera.
`ReplayEdit` cuts it like a television director, from the same tape and the same seed to the same edit, in shots of 2.6 to 9 s: it opens on the grid, cuts to every fall (the bike is shown lying on the asphalt, the others riding past), to chosen overtakes and battles, to the player's laps, and ends with the winner and the
player crossing the line. The cameras:

## Co-op

Up to four people race together; the others are computer riders on the host's machine. Every machine rides its own bike with its own physics (nobody waits for the network to steer) and sends its pose twenty
times a second; the host relays everybody's pose to everybody, gives the go and says how the race ended.

1. The host picks `Su pista` from `Ospita una partita` and `Via`. The `Welcome` tells a friend that the game is a race (and the seed); the friend builds the circuit and the scenery from the seed, says it is ready (`FloorReady`,
   as in the rest of the co-op) and receives the roster: laps, bike, aids and hour of the **host**, and every rider in the host's order with colours and a place on the grid. A friend's bike is a rider without a physics
   model here, drawn easing toward the pose that arrives; the computer riders of the host are the same on a friend's machine.
2. The host waits for every friend to have built the circuit and for `INVIO` / `A` (the status line at the bottom says who is still loading), then the lights start on every machine.
3. Whoever joins while the race is on the grid takes the next place at the back; whoever comes in after the start waits for the next race.
4. The end is the host's: the host's rules say when the race is over and send the results (places, times, falls, who retired), and every machine shows that order. A friend who leaves retires and the race does not wait for them;
   if the host goes, the race ends where it is ("Connessione persa") and a new one can be started alone.
5. After the results the host chooses another race on the same circuit or a new one; the friends build it again (a new epoch of the same game).

The map shows a dot for every rider; a friend has the colour of their bike and a white ring. The messages (protocol `pe-coop-35`) are `PistaPose` (a friend's pose, to the host), `PistaRoster`, `PistaStart`,
`PistaPoses` (everybody's poses, unreliable) and `PistaOver` (the results), all in `Scripts/Pista/PistaNet.cs` and `Scripts/Coop/CoopWire.cs`. A pose is about 35 bytes, so the nineteen riders of a full grid
(fifteen computer riders, the host and three friends) fit in one packet of 665 bytes; whatever arrives is checked by `NetReader` and a pose outside the world is thrown away. The replay and the ghost belong to each machine: they are not sent. Contact between bikes works across machines (see "Contact between bikes"): each machine moves its own bike when a friend's pose touches it.

  - The driving line and the braking sign in the friend's process: `tools/coop/run_all.ps1` runs `race` with `-pistaaids 1` on the host and `-pistacoopaids 1` on both. The friend chose `Completi` itself and must play with the host's `Sportivi`, which the roster carries; each side checks that the aids it rides with are the host's, that its line is there and shows only the red, and that its braking sign says what its line says on every frame (three checks more on each side: 26 and 23 in all, 112 s).
  Seed 4242 is the circuit *Autodromo di Montesole*, 2,732 m; the race of one lap takes 83 s.

## Not verified, and decisions left to the developer

- Bikes touching was set from numbers and never felt: the radius (0.4 m), the speeds that separate a nudge from a bump and from a fall (1.2, 6 and 9.5 m/s) and the three seconds of grace are mine. A touch has no sound (the camera shakes and the screen says `CONTATTO`), a bike lying on the track is not an obstacle (riders go through it) and computer riders do not touch each other.
- Not played by hand: the feel of the physics, of the cameras and of the controls on a keyboard and on a controller is judged only by numbers and by pictures. The controller mapping (the race and the replay) has never been tried with a physical controller. The keyboard was measured with an emulated rider (the `keyboard` group of the lab): it shows how the keys compare with a stick and that the cap and the lean assist keep that rider on the bike, not how the ramps and the cap feel to a person; the speeds and the 70% of the cap were chosen on that rider.
- The sounds (the nine engines, tyres, kerbs, wind, the start beeps) are made by code, measured in the lab and in the game, and never listened to.
- The replay was judged from its photographs and the checks of the cameras, not watched in motion: how the cuts, the speed of a pan or the whip of the low camera *feel* is not verified.
- The co-op was run only between two processes on one PC, never over a real network, and with the computer riding the bikes of both machines; three friends at once (a full table) is covered by the rules lab and the size of a packet, not by a run.
- The way into a co-op race through the menu (`Ospita una partita`, `Su pista`, `Via`) is read from the code but no test walks it: the co-op runs choose the mode with `-pista`.
- Split screen on one PC does not offer this mode.
- The desert's motorbike was not changed: the new bike model, mesh and sound belong to this mode alone. Whether the desert should use them is a decision for the developer.
- The medal times of the licence tests are tied to the racing line of the computer riders and checked against computer riders only; whether a person can reach silver and gold, and whether three laps are the right number, is not known.
- The garage screen was judged from photographs of the showroom of two bikes (the 1000 and the 125, the second with a rider in other colours than its bike) and from a test that presses its buttons and reads its texts and the colours of the materials; nobody has used it with a controller or watched the plate turn. The scales of the bars (a full power bar is 210 hp, a full torque bar 170 Nm, a full speed bar 320 km/h and an empty one 150, a full mass bar 120 kg and an empty one 330) are a choice made so that the nine bikes spread along them, not a measurement, and the weights that make the handling word (70 % the rate of roll, 30 % the grip) are a judgment that no rider has tested.
- What the mode keeps: the ghosts of the best laps on the named circuits, in files of their own, the book of licence times, one line of text in the preferences, and the rows of the setup (the bike and the colours of the garage among them). The championship adds the book of its seasons and trophies to that, and the career the book of its money and bikes. It has no saves of characters; no circuit, licence or cup is locked behind another, and with the career off no bike is either (with it on a bike has a price). A tuning shop by parts (tyres, gear ratios, suspension: Tourist Trophy has one) is not made: the workshop of the career sells three packages for each bike.
- The championship was never played by a person: the three levels of the rivals were set against the computer riders of the lab (a rider at 0.97 of the pace wins a round, one at 0.86 is last), not against a human, so whether `Principiante` is gentle enough, whether a trophy is within reach at `Campione` and whether four rounds of three laps are the right length is not known. The points are those of a real championship and the calendars, the three classes with a cup and the three without are a choice. The rivals of a season are fixed by the cup and the level when it began; they do not learn and do not get better round by round. A race in progress is not saved. The panel was judged from photographs and from a test that presses its buttons, with no controller.
- The career was never played: the starting money, the prices, the purses and the trophies are numbers I chose, and the lab checks only their order and the pace (the first step up is within reach of three golds, a win is about a twelfth of it, the licences alone do not buy every bike), not how it feels to earn them. Whether 3,000 cr is the right start, whether a dozen wins for a 600 is the right grind, whether a win at `Principiante` should pay so much less than one at `Campione`, and whether the rivals should earn too, is not known. The levels of tuning and their prices are numbers I chose too: the lab measured what a full tune does to a lap (about 3 % for a rider who uses the grip, less than 2 % for the computer riders), not how it feels, whether it is worth 41 % of a bike or whether three levels are enough, and the rivals are never tuned, so a tuned bike is a head start against them, by design. A bike cannot be sold, tuning is only the three packages (no parts, no gear ratios), the career cannot be started over from the menu and a race in progress is not saved. The garage with its `Carriera`, `Compra` and `Potenzia` was judged from four photographs and from a test that presses its buttons and reads their texts, with no controller. And the way to the real preferences was never run: the automated runs keep the preferences in memory, so what is written after a purchase and read again at the next start is read from the code only (it was by reading that the first version of the book was found to share the key of the on/off switch, a preference holding one value, and the key was changed).
- The driving line was never ridden with by a person. Its colours and the figures behind them (3.5 m/s2 for a braking zone, 0.97 and 1.05 for the red and the green, 90 % for the depth of a red, 12 m of lead, 10 m for the least piece, 300 m of ribbon, 42 cm of width) are mine, and were judged from the lab, from the test that reads the ribbon on the grid and during the race and from photographs (the standing start, the race at speed from the three cameras, the red coming up, the yellow of a corner); how it feels to brake on a red that grows shorter, whether the red comes too early or too late for a person, whether the ribbon is too bright or too thin and whether the helmet camera needs it at all is not known. It shows the plan of the computer riders, which is made with a fixed grip: for a tuned bike the plan knows the extra power and the lighter weight and the smaller drag but not the extra grip, so on a tuned bike the red comes a little early. In a cooperative game each machine builds the same code for its own rider with the aids of the host (run between two processes with the host at `Sportivi`, which the friend, at `Completi` by its own choice, takes over: its line shows only the red and its sign follows the line; one lap, one PC, never a real network). It makes no sound (there is no beep at the braking point) and it is not an ideal line for a person: it is the line the computer riders ride. From the chase camera the rider hides the ribbon when it runs straight under him (in the photograph at 96 km/h the red ahead is hidden by the rider, and at 202 km/h the red 78 m ahead is only a thin orange sliver at the horizon); the far camera and the helmet camera show it whole. The braking sign was made for that and says the same from any camera (the photographs show it at 202 km/h, `FRENA TRA 80 m`, and at 96 km/h on the red, `FRENA`). It has the same limits and some of its own: its five seconds, its 60 m, its rounding, the 5 % that ends a dip and the 800 m it reads are mine; it mirrors the ribbon, so a rider who brakes earlier than the plan sees the red move away and the countdown can grow again; the computer rider brakes before the red reaches it, so in a whole race the order `FRENA` came up on 2 frames of 103,473 and is checked by hand from the grid (a bike set at 71 m/s on the red) more than by a ride; it makes no sound; and where it stands (bottom, left of the middle, 310 by 80 units) was judged from photographs at 1600 by 900, not on a small screen. Nobody has braked by it.
